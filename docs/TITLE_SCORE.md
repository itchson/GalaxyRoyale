# Native Galaxy Royale title score

The supplied `Galaxy Royale (3).mp3` is an offline reference owned by the user. Its SHA256 is `95fcb149bb55da752c4d93c18fcc1913a23b70ccafecb52d8db5548dc64755d6`; measured duration is 42.680 seconds. It is not copied into the game, decoded at runtime or used as a playback source. The current authored score is a reference-inspired draft, not an accepted exact transcription.

The existing browser WebAudio AudioEngine performs synthesis. Rust publishes authoritative world/area/threat intent; the browser batches that intent into one shared transport, music bus and 32-voice music budget. This is not a claim that DSP runs inside Rust. Oscillator/noise notes, authored rests, phrase data and timbres are maintained in `title-theme.ts` and `music-score.ts`.

Machine analysis suggests B minor and a 169–170 BPM pulse family, with a half-time ambiguity. Notes/octaves/chord changes still need listening checks. The initial authored title has 512 sixteenth steps (32 bars, about 45.37 seconds); this differs from the source form. Its synthesis, tempo transitions and perceived fidelity are separate acceptance criteria.

Continue preserves the current beat and harmonic phase while fading into the selected corporation's Haven arrangement and decelerating over 8 beats to 120 BPM. Actual hostile combat intent accelerates over 4 beats to 166 BPM. Leaving combat holds 2 beats before an 8-beat return to 120. Re-entry retargets the existing phase and ramp. Boss, champion, biome, corporate, propulsion and low-hull layers use the same engine. Browser audio starts only after a gesture; hidden/interrupted/muted lifecycle remains bounded.

The pure beat clock integrates tempo ramps rather than abruptly changing timer intervals. The scheduler has a 0.18-second lookahead and at most 16 grid steps per tick. Missed grid steps are skipped after short or long stalls, so past onsets cannot bunch together on recovery. Future voices are canceled and rescheduled at changes; already audible chord tails follow the retimed harmonic boundary. Every voice is released on disposal.

The first real audition received the feedback “Instrument sound and mix.” A second pass added held lead/bass articulation and sustained pads; its native title RMS increased by 11.55 dB without changing the buses. The user then said “It still needs fuller instruments.” The third pass adds a finite detuned stereo lead pair plus nominal warm body, eight-layer chord pads and centered bass harmonics. World music, SFX and thrust retain their existing routing and envelopes. Panners are optional; unavailable panners use the mono route. Each layer belongs to a bounded voice and is stopped/disconnected with it.

Actual evidence is in `artifacts/title-score-20261013/`: source analysis, specialist source/clock tests, independent review and three native browser DSP captures. Audition MP3s there are compressed exports of newly rendered engine PCM for development review, not source recordings or shipped game assets. All seven fixtures in each capture were finite, had no samples at or beyond unity and cleaned up every voice/callback. The third 48-second title render peaks at 0.157576 with RMS 0.024680; stereo correlation is 0.988069 and its mono-downmix RMS loss is 0.0261 dB. Its RMS is 1.01 dB below V2: timbre/width refinement is not a loudness increase. The shared clock retains measured title/Haven/chase/release ramps. These results do not establish exact melody recognition, human comfort, physical-device performance or final mix acceptance. The third audition is awaiting direct feedback.
