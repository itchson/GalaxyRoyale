# Current full-Game native render profile

The pinned production `Game::render_into` path completed all declared fixtures. On this Ryzen 9 9950X3D Windows desktop, the maximum observed process-cold render was **2.211 ms**. The highest per-fixture warm p95/p99 was **0.339 / 0.365 ms**, in the deliberately extreme 8192×8192 world extent. This is CPU extraction evidence, not a browser, GPU, phone, sustained-frame-rate, or 30-minute-soak result.

## Exact inputs and method

Production `cosmic_background.rs` SHA256 `dee899be884b308fe02d447743f58b7ca3125b53a1cef39c0be78f97f40e1023`; production `lib.rs` SHA256 `b79373610953196fb946467ae280a96e1041878187654cdf20b02c6bba887f62`. All 46 archived source/asset/manifest/lock inputs are listed in `source-pins.json`. They were checked before compilation, after compilation, after measurement, and before archival. No production, shared manifest, or dist files were edited.

`cargo build -p game_core --release --locked --target-dir work/cosmic-render-profile/native-target --message-format=json` compiled the actual production library and engine dependencies. The standalone public-API `probe.rs` linked those exact rlibs, with `rustc --edition=2024 -C opt-level=3 -C lto=thin`. `compiled-inputs.json` identifies emitted rlibs; `conditions.json` records compiler, OS, timestamps and machine conditions. The package and probe use the existing release opt-level 3 / thin-LTO settings; this is neither WASM nor the earlier custom-module/debug-dependency proof.

There are **48 fixtures, three independent child processes each**, yielding 144 process-cold renders and **86,400 timed warm renders**. Each process separately times `Game::new`, scene setup, the first `render_into` into a fresh `FrameBuilder`, then 60 untimed warmups and 600 timed extractions into the reused builder. New/setup costs are excluded from the reported cold-render interval. Windows `Instant` supplies wall-clock timings. OS/file caches, affinity, frequency, other app activity and thermal state were not controlled; a fresh process resets Rust `LazyLock` state, not hardware or disk caches.

All scenes use the actual default seed `0x47524f59` and starting Scout. Haven runs four idle 60Hz ticks. Other scenic fixtures use the production public finite `warp` diagnostic, then four idle ticks. That public path streams actual asteroid bodies, refreshes actual sites, suppresses QA rewards and normal wave spawning. The combat fixture uses `diagnostic("combat")`, which authors nine ordinary enemy heads, then four actual ticks; ordinary asteroid streaming and a natural spawn also run. Its measured snapshot has 64 ECS entities, including segmented bodies. No artificial body-count-only rendering is substituted.

Four ticks place the warp's existing invulnerability blink in its visible interval. They do not render scenery, so its lazy caches remain process-cold. Simulation time is then held fixed during measurement: these are repeated full production **snapshot extractions**, not an advancing combat simulation, and not samples of tick, input, HUD serialization or AI cost. Initial time-zero exploratory probes, which omitted the deliberately blinking ship, are preserved in `exploratory-time-zero-*` and excluded from the final table and totals.

Logical extents 533×300 use accepted zoom-derived world extents at 0.6, 1.0 and 1.6 for Haven, outer Aurora Reach, centered galaxy, nebula and supernova fixtures, plus combat. Desktop 1200×700 tests outer/supernova at both zoom limits. Two 8192×8192 far-world fixtures exercise the accepted extraction extent bound at [999000, −999000]. Two galaxy fixtures additionally use Reduced Motion. Full/reduced cosmetics are paired throughout. `set_viewport(logical / zoom)` reflects the production camera contract; the native renderer/GPU does not execute the zoom shader in this test.

The full path includes custom cosmic layers, actual seeded regions, site beacons, campaign geometry when applicable, the Haven dock, and the ordinary live actor/bullet/loot/particle extraction paths. These fixtures have no transient particles, pickups or boss encounter. Browser semantic pixel UI, GPU packing/upload, lighting, presentation shake and rasterization are excluded. The timed call really visits those world extraction paths; separate `regions::background_with_density` packet counts are measured only afterward and outside the timing window.

## Measured results

Nearest-rank percentiles pool 1,800 warm samples per fixture; the three cold samples are reported as median/min/max, never as an invented statistically meaningful cold p99. Raw microseconds are in `samples.json`; `summary.json`/`summary.csv` include every fixture, creation/setup times and warm maxima. The complete [fixture table](fixture-table.md) includes all 48 rows.

| Declared group | Highest cold observation ms | Highest fixture warm p95 / p99 ms | Maximum emitted quads |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 533×300 logical snapshots | 1.287 | 0.0945 / 0.1108 | 22,563 |
| 1200×700 logical snapshots | 1.773 | 0.1644 / 0.1986 | 33,409 |
| 8192×8192 far-world extent | 2.211 | 0.3394 / 0.3654 | 41,688 |

The highest individual warm observation was 0.4462 ms. Cold median across individual fixtures ranged 0.1014–2.1778 ms. Process-fresh `Game::new` observations ranged 0.6073–0.7120 ms, with per-fixture medians 0.6190–0.6860 ms. Setup, including the declared actual ticks and streamed geometry, is retained separately in the raw evidence. These separate intervals cannot be described as complete game launch or total frame timings.

At far full quality, the real frame has **41,688 quads, 196 transforms, two batches**, including 31,194 lighting-responsive body cells and 6,000 region scenery quads. Reduced quality emits **36,389 quads, 198 transforms, two batches**, including exactly the same 31,194 body cells and 3,000 region quads. Different transform counts can result from different surviving scenery groups; quality is not a promise to halve total world cells or transform records. At desktop supernova/minimum zoom, the frame contains 33,409 quads and 126 transforms in one batch.

The initial and final complete packet digests match in every process, and all three processes per fixture emit identical packet digests/counts. Initial packet validation checks all values finite, every pixel's transform index valid, exact `pixel_count`, and each batch at or below 128 transform slots. The profile is unchanged by the full measured render sequence. The harness does not hash every warm frame inside its timed interval. No broad workspace test suite was rerun or claimed here.

## Cold initialization and scenery omission

Source-derived, not a process-heap measurement: the new cosmic `LazyLock<Art>` constructs exactly six full and six reduced cached `PixelBody` templates on first visible motif demand. Full dimensions are 96×96, 24×24, 80×48, 48×24, 64×64 and 12×12; reduced dimensions halve both axes. That is **23,780 stored color slots**, plus **374 u64 occupancy words**: 98,112 bytes for those persistent arrays, before 12 body headers, LazyLock overhead and temporary authoring strings/vectors. Dimensions and loops are fixed, unrelated to travel distance, seed population or accumulated frame count. Templates are constructed together once; full/reduced body changes reuse the cache. First visible motifs and first eligible region planets can therefore create a cold spike even after a simpler Haven frame has previously rendered.

The reported cold interval includes any applicable cosmic **and existing region** lazy initialization, fresh builder allocation and actual full extraction. It is not a timer inside the cosmic initializer, so no precise fraction of that interval is attributed to one cache. There is no RSS, allocation-count or long-session memory bound claim. The measured bounded scenes, plus fixed cache dimensions, support retaining this implementation for the next browser/device measurements.

Custom cosmic scenery has its own 8,000/4,000 submitted-quad cap, including reserved star maxima 1,800/900. Whole galaxy/nebula/supernova groups are admitted atomically against the remainder. Existing region/Scar scenery has a separate 6,000/3,000 cap and earlier-layer priority. Faint, overlapping, or alpha-zero occupied cells still consume submitted-cell budgets. These caps do not apply to gameplay bodies, sites, the Haven ring, UI or particles.

Both extreme far fixtures **actually reach the exact 6,000/3,000 region cap**, so later region scenery is deliberately omitted. Huge viewports can exhaust the custom star reserve, causing later field/layer stars to be omitted, or leave too little motif budget for another complete group. This is finite quality admission, not a guarantee every seed-selected object is visible. In particular, the existing ordinary region planet templates have about 4,300–4,500 occupied cells each, so none can fit a reduced region budget of 3,000; that behavior predates the new cosmic module. At Haven/minimum zoom, actual full region extraction emits 5,204 cells while reduced emits 400. This can affect visible scenery substantially despite unchanged deterministic world identities.

Foreground streaming also remains the existing bounded local chunk neighborhood: the 8192 fixture does not populate its entire large viewport with additional chunks. Its counts are actual emitted packets of that current Game scene, not a claim about all possible dense-wave/terrain combinations. Sustained simulation, crowded Motes, animated travelling cache misses, cold WASM startup, browser allocation/GC, audible audio, GPU and physical Pixel 6a/iPhone 12 performance remain Pending.

## Reproduction and freeze

Before freezing, `python work/cosmic-render-profile/run.py` built and measured the declared current-source fixtures; `summarize.py` summarized and archived verified inputs. Those scripts write output and must not be rerun over this accepted freeze. `python work/cosmic-render-profile/verify.py` is read-only and verifies the owned artifact manifest and archived source hashes. Use `--current-source` additionally only when checking whether the checkout still exactly matches this historical measurement. The large native target remains work-only; its rlib hashes are documented separately, while the archived inputs and exact commands preserve provenance.
