# Galaxy Royale

A single-player neo-retro space shooter built from portable Rust simulation and destructible pixel fields. The game and local production dashboard are separate applications.

## Run locally

Requirements: Rust with `wasm32-unknown-unknown`, Node 24+, and pnpm. Windows PowerShell is supported.

```powershell
pnpm install --frozen-lockfile
pnpm build:wasm
pnpm dev
```

The WASM script installs the exact `wasm-bindgen-cli` version in `Cargo.lock` when required, then builds separate WebGPU and WebGL2 bundles. Both complete binding sets use content-addressed URLs recorded in a validated manifest; Vite embeds that exact pair. Build WASM before starting or restarting a development server.

- Game: http://127.0.0.1:4173
- Mission Control: http://127.0.0.1:4175
- Local production API: http://127.0.0.1:4174

Use `pnpm build` for the complete release build, including both WASM backends, TypeScript checks, game assets, the game-only service worker, and the dashboard. The deployable game is `dist/`; the dashboard is `apps/studio/dist/` and is never included in that release.

## Fly

Desktop: left click sets a persistent destination; holding left steers toward the cursor and continuously thrusts along the ship's actual heading, even near the cursor. Release restores destination braking. Hold right to turn toward the cursor and fire; cursor aim follows the current unshaken world projection as the camera and zoom move. Right aim overrides facing while held-left thrust follows that facing; released click courses retain arrival braking. Turning remains smooth at 720°/second, and shots leave the current nose with the existing quarter-velocity inheritance. Scroll the mouse wheel to smoothly zoom from 0.6× to 1.6×, while pixel UI stays at screen size. WASD/arrows are available too; Space does not fire. Escape toggles a live ship-status menu: it consumes controls and cancels the old course while the universe continues. Its pixel icons continue flight, restart at Haven, open Settings or return to the title. Left click or tap a Haven facility to open its dedicated Forge, Hangar, Journal or Arenas page. E is an optional Forge shortcut at Haven and interacts with a nearby character outside it. Right fire does not open a menu. The compact flight toolbar opens Ship Status (Escape), the discovery map (M), or Settings. The map shows only discovered seeded beacons, sanctuary and selected resource-belt radii; pan/zoom and Set Course navigate real world coordinates. Settings includes a title-screen icon.

Mobile: rotate to landscape. The left joystick steers and applies proportional thrust; the right button fires independently. Release the stick to coast with a short throttle fade. Fullscreen and separate music/effects volume controls are available.

Haven protects the ship within 240 units. The asteroid belt begins at 700; combat begins beyond 1,400. Moving farther increases danger. Darts weave, Bastions strafe and fire, and Coils use articulated overlapping segment joints, including their boss hulls. Twenty-four kills in one family queue its first boss; every two defeats of a boss generation queue its successor, through generation five. All fifteen encounters have authored pixel silhouettes, distinct attacks and charging/attack/exposed-core phases. One ordinary boss is active at a time. Retreat, death and reload preserve outstanding encounters and surplus kill credit.

Each ship earns its own XP and keeps six hardware ranks plus exactly three automatic ability slots: offensive, defensive and passive. Owned craftable abilities and their ranks are shared inventory; each hull keeps a compatible loadout, and effective ability rank is capped by that ship's level. The Forge shows slots first, with a separate category editor and Back action. Hardware ranks cost ten matching points plus one banked material of the next rank's tier. Ability starters cost three matching points plus two Tier 1 materials; other normal abilities cost ten plus two Tier 1 to craft, then ten plus two materials of the next rank's tier to upgrade. Crafting acquires an item; Equip explicitly installs or replaces it. Rank gates are ship levels 1/3/5/8/12.

The Scout starts at 100 units/second and 260 acceleration, gaining 2.5% drive per level through level 12, plus purchased drive ranks and any equipped drive ability. Belt asteroids born between 700 and 1,400 units grant no XP and can drop basic category points and Tier 1 materials. Canonical remote sanctuaries have their own seeded resource annuli with distance-based higher tiers (the map reports recommended ship levels). Belt rocks follow gentle orbital motion, keeping real knockback and contact impulses. Haven and remote sanctuary shields reflect every asteroid outside their protection radius, including fast swept crossings; shield impacts never award loot or XP. Family bosses can unlock Pulse Echo, Repulsor and Ion Wake blueprints; the fourth eligible defeat guarantees the family blueprint. These effects require crafting and the matching equipped slot. Collected points, ship XP, owned equipment and dock stash survive death. Five-tier crafting cargo occupies a 16-unit hold and banks only on physical arrival at Haven; death, restart, return to title and reload discard carried materials. Death returns the ship to Haven with an expedition report and Retry/Return to Title; arrival never automatically opens a menu. Live enemies, uncollected loot and asteroid damage remain transient.

Eight seeded arenas contain champions with authored pixel hulls and distinct attacks. Defeating a champion awards its patch and hull/ability blueprints once. At Haven, hull construction costs ten construction-category points and six banked Tier 1 materials; crafting an ability is a separate transaction. Champion abilities fit their authored hull family, while starter and ordinary boss abilities fit all owned hulls. The Hangar shows each owned ship's own XP. All eight patches reveal the Galaxy Royale gate: eight player duels and 28 explicitly simulated NPC pairings form a nine-entrant round robin. First place unlocks the Sovereign and three equipped rank-five royal bundles once. A newly awarded Sovereign starts with zero XP/level 1 and its explicit enhanced chassis; unequipping a royal bundle disables that bundle's effects. A losing season can be retried. See `docs/CAMPAIGN.md` for the catalog, rules and checkpoint boundaries.

A seeded, jittered grid samples six biomes: Haven Expanse, Amethyst Drift, Aurora Reach, Iron Belt, Radial Wastes, and Cryo Rift. Protected discovery sites beyond Haven offer courier deliveries and enemy-family bounties. Accept an event, fulfil it, and return to its source to claim three matching points and a unique relic. Each relic adds one maximum hull, capped at twenty. Discoveries, quests, claimed rewards and the equipment inventory persist in schema-4 saves; older saves migrate without losing recorded currency/XP/counters. Legacy scalar crafting cargo and storage migrate once to Tier 1 dock stash; schema-4 cargo is discarded on reload rather than banked. Courier mission packets remain separate from crafting cargo and survive death/reload. Iron Belt Riveters and Radial Wastes Motes are implemented; environmental radiation hazards remain future content.

Title, menu, HUD, sliders, toggles, and buttons render through Rust pixel quads. Generated artwork is converted into hard-alpha, palette-indexed cells; it is never drawn as a sprite texture. RoyaleMono uses authored 5×7 glyph bodies with fixed character spacing and grouped transforms. The invisible semantic DOM retains accessible controls and pointer capture; the generated RoyaleMono TTF matches the engine glyph metrics for semantic layout; Tiny5 and its OFL license remain bundled as fallback. Settings are available from the title and live ship menu, with separate audio buses, camera shake, reduced motion, and cosmetic density. Live telemetry shows the selected hull's XP, effective hardware statistics, three equipped ability ranks, carried tiers and retained stash/points. Death replaces any live menu with the expedition report. Quit on the title commits and flushes progression before requesting browser close; ordinary tabs may require the user to close the tab/app themselves.

The flight HUD uses compact page icons and one quiet bottom status strip. Small hull/shield tracks follow the interpolated ship through the unshaken camera; safe-area and actual hitbox reservations move vital summaries into the strip when those tracks cannot fit. Low hull and depleted shields have distinct native warnings; reduced motion removes their color pulse. Contextual contacts and transient notices share one bounded text slot. Detailed points, hardware, abilities and inventory remain in Ship Status. Backtick opens a black drawer at the top, sliding down over 180 ms; Escape or backtick closes it. Reduced motion disables the slide. `HELP`, `STATS`, `PROFILE`, and `RENDERER` report actual runtime data, `CLEAR` clears its output, and Up/Down recalls commands. The debug console and conversations intentionally suspend simulation; closing them reconciles current title/death/hidden-tab/renderer gates. Escape and launched Settings/Forge/other game menus remain live, with movement and firing gated beneath them. Hidden tabs suspend simulation and clear interrupted controls/course; visibility restoration resumes the eligible world with menus intact. `RECOVER` requests graphics recovery. The HUD hides while console or dialogue modals are open.

Press E at a discovery sanctuary to open its contextual conversation at the bottom, with an authored pixel portrait, the site's actual event description and an eligible accept/claim action. The panel pauses flight and restores the previous play state on close. Accepting an event and retaining its quest through offline reload were verified in the browser. Native tests verify courier/bounty rewards claim once and survive reload; completing and claiming the reward through this panel still needs a browser play test.

Pixel materials provide per-object light response and emission. A maximum of eight camera-relative local lights shades each occupied cell while UI and background materials stay unlit. Hard world quads use a backing-resolution sRGB target; pixel UI retains its own logical-resolution, nearest-composited target. Fractional geometry is preserved through affine transforms and zoom, without a hard-quad size floor. Soft gas/fire cells use bounded analytic coverage filtering, area-compensated alpha and emission rather than sprite textures. Arbitrarily faint sub-screen shapes still have physical sampling limits. Seeded gas clouds, moving star systems and constellations use bounded parallax groups; engine-port fire, projectile trails and debris use the bounded particle pool with separate cosmetic hashing. Reduced density changes cosmetics only. Desktop and physical-device acceptance are recorded separately for each build.

The authored Web Audio score blends Haven and exploration identities, biome arrangements, actual hostile pursuit, individual champion themes and a low-hull warning layer. Thrust pitch/level follows real speed and throttle; projectiles, shield loss, impacts and equipped abilities have synthesized cues. Music/effects have separate buses and bounded voices, with hidden-tab and renderer recovery cleanup. Deterministic mix and recorded graph tests verify behavior; human listening and physical-device audio remain separate acceptance. See `docs/STYLE_SYSTEM.md` for the coherent palette, pixel detail and restrained UI contracts.

## Architecture

- `crates/engine_core`: packed cell occupancy, affine hierarchy, swept cell collision, local destruction, bounded pixel particles, reusable render batches.
- `crates/game_core`: `hecs` object ECS, fixed-step physics, deterministic streamed chunks/regions, protected sites, quests, enemies, bosses, loot, progression, compiled occupied pixel artwork.
- `crates/game_web`: `wgpu` instanced quads and low-resolution nearest presentation; browser WASM interface, fixed 60 Hz accumulator, interpolated extraction, device recovery.
- `apps/game`: pointer controls, HUD, procedural Web Audio score/SFX, versioned IndexedDB snapshots, import/export, and PWA lifecycle.
- `apps/studio`: localhost Node API, atomic versioned project document, six-state board, reviews, builds, decisions, activity, feedback queue, and agent CLI.

See `docs/CONTRACTS.md`, `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md`, `docs/ENGINE.md`, `docs/DEBUG_CONSOLE.md`, `docs/CAMPAIGN.md`, `docs/NARRATIVE.md`, `docs/ROADMAP.md`, and `apps/studio/README.md`. `production/project.json` contains actual recorded assignments, acceptance criteria, reviews, and evidence. Only the running API writes it. Queued feedback is read during active development sessions; the dashboard does not launch autonomous sessions.

## Validate

```powershell
cargo test --workspace --locked
cargo clippy -p engine_core -p game_core --all-targets -- -D warnings
pnpm test
pnpm test:wasm-contract
pnpm typecheck
pnpm build
```

Add `?qa=1` to enable explicit test scenes and the read-only `window.galaxyQA.report()` capture. The scene toolbar and performance overlay are hidden by default: add `&toolbar=1` or `&metrics=1` to show them. In QA mode, the backtick console accepts `SCENE dock|belt|combat|site|biome-riveter|boss-dart[-1..5]|boss-bastion[-1..5]|boss-coil[-1..5]|champion-0..7|tournament|stress|die`; close it to resume a running session. The boss aliases without a suffix select generation one. `?renderer=webgl2` forces fallback; `?qa=1&fail-webgpu=1` rejects the first initializer and tests automatic fallback. `?qa=1&scene=stress` exercises at least 80,000 visible quads, 160 enemy heads, 300 projectiles, and 2,000 particles. `?qa=1&touch=1` exposes mobile controls for desktop layout checks; this does not validate physical touch hardware. Diagnostics do not award free progression. The console's `STATS` command also reports last rendered boss panel bounds.

Desktop packaged-game offline reload and launch were verified after stopping the local preview server: the title, WASM runtime and flight loaded from the service-worker cache. Phone-specific installation, updates and lifecycle acceptance remain pending.

The WASM contract check builds isolated QA packages under `work/` and validates eight actual Rust fresh/champion/generation/legacy-boss snapshots through the TypeScript schema and IndexedDB transactions for both backends. Optional Rust values serialize as explicit `null`; a fresh ship's empty run history must remain a valid save. QA fixture exports are excluded from the shipped bundles.

## Validation boundaries and next content

The Iron Belt includes the occupied-cell Riveter with destructible barrels and a locked triple burst. Radial Wastes Motes use a charged escape wedge and two radial pulses; moving/crowded warning readability and human pacing remain separate playtest gates. Boss health/phase panels adapt around projected cores, ships and fixed controls, including compact minimal telemetry. See `docs/BIOME_ENEMIES.md`, `docs/BOSS_HUD.md` and the dated `artifacts/` records for implementation and each build's acceptance limits. Historical browser/native records do not automatically validate the new schema-4 progression or crafting views.

All fifteen family bosses and five-generation promotion are implemented. Independent native projectile fights cover all fifteen, with full-profile diagnostic isolation and a naturally queued fifth-generation retreat/reload/death/retry. Human balance, a complete natural progression playthrough and full browser campaign acceptance remain pending. Invite-based co-op, additional biome enemies, and radiation hazards remain backlog. Accepted future scope also includes player-owned docks with upgradeable protection radii and arenas, plus a separate multiplayer battle royale: a compact seeded circular universe, equally spaced zones, arena sieges and a last-player-standing outcome. Its edge crossing must place a ship at the antipodal edge while preserving heading and speed and rebasing the camera. A portable, opt-in `CircularTopology` primitive implements and tests that motion/rebase behavior; the solo game does not use it. Owned docks, arena modes, seeded multiplayer zones, network authority and co-op remain future work. See `docs/ROADMAP.md` and Mission Control for the backlog and unresolved rules.

Disconnected asteroid islands do not yet become independent rigid-body fragments; removed cells become bounded debris. Simulation coordinates are bounded to a practical range; extreme exploration requires a future floating-origin migration.

Physical Pixel 6a/iPhone 12 performance, sustained 30-minute mobile soak, Safari compatibility, and device-specific audio/PWA lifecycle remain pending until measured on those devices. Desktop measurements are labelled separately in `artifacts/` and Mission Control. Cosmetic density should be reduced before gameplay budgets change.

The optional offline ACE-Step experiment is not required by the game. No generated audio candidate has been accepted. The shipped score and effects are authored procedural Web Audio; the generated concept sheet is a direction reference, not a gameplay screenshot.
